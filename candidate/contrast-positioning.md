# Contrast Without a Name — MO-02 Primary Positioning

How Matt Grant draws a sharp contrast in the August 4, 2026 Republican primary **without ever
naming the incumbent** — by making his own strengths the standard the race is measured against.
The opponent is never the subject of a sentence; the *gap* is. Voters draw the conclusion.

> **Guardrails (non-negotiable).** No fabricated or paraphrased quotes attributed to anyone. No
> personal or derogatory characterizations. Any factual claim about a public record needs a
> `source_url` before publish (see `references/ethics-and-guardrails.md`). This file is strategy, not
> opposition research, and not legal advice — have election counsel confirm disclaimers and
> compliance. Numbers below are illustrative planning placeholders.

---

## 1. The core move: "be the answer, not the attack"

Primary voters rarely reward a challenger for tearing down a known name — but they reward a
challenger who makes the familiar feel *insufficient*. So every message states a **standard Matt
meets** that an entrenched record implicitly does not. The contrast lives in the voter's head, not
on Matt's lips.

| Common incumbent vulnerability (unnamed) | Matt's strength that answers it | How it's said (no name) |
|---|---|---|
| Long tenure / career politician | **Term limits** (with grandfather clause); fresh problem-solver | "Service was never meant to be a career. I'll vote to limit my own." |
| Distance / doesn't show up | **Neighbor, dad, shows up** | "I'll hold open town halls in every county — and answer the hard questions." |
| Partisanship / gridlock, few results | **Across-the-aisle litigator who gets results** | "Twenty-three years winning cases by building coalitions. I deliver, then explain." |
| Cost of living / spending | **Smaller government, lower taxes, cut waste first** | "Relief funded by efficiency, not gimmicks. Cut the waste, then the taxes." |
| Nothing distinctive to show for the seat | **Children first / CHILD Protection Act** | "One urgent problem no one else will take on: clean up the courts and put kids first." |

Note: referencing **"more than a decade"** or **"career politicians"** as a *category* is fair,
factual, and name-free. Use sparingly and always pivot back to what Matt will do.

---

## 2. Message architecture (strengths-forward)

- **North-star:** *A neighbor, a dad, and a problem-solver — putting Missouri's children first.*
- **Pillar 1 — Fresh, accountable service.** Term limits, including his own. (Contrast: tenure.)
- **Pillar 2 — Results across the aisle.** 23 years getting outcomes, not headlines. (Contrast: gridlock.)
- **Pillar 3 — Children first.** The CHILD Protection Act — the signature cause that's uniquely his.
- **Pillar 4 — Affordability through discipline.** Cut fraud, waste, and headcount; then cut taxes.

Each pillar is a *forward promise*, never a backward swipe. The implied comparison is structural:
"new energy," "I'll show up," "I'll limit my own term," "the work no one else is leading."

---

## 3. Phrase bank — name-free contrast language

Use these; they all pass the "no-name" test and stay factual:

- "After more than a decade, families in our district are still waiting on the basics."
- "I'm not running to hold a seat. I'm running to fix something specific."
- "Career politicians manage the clock. I've spent 23 years beating it for clients."
- "You shouldn't need a fundraiser invitation to reach your member of Congress."
- "The strongest argument for term limits is how rarely they're offered by the people in office."
- "Fresh energy isn't a slogan — it's a willingness to limit your own power."

Avoid: anything that puts the incumbent in the subject position, any invented quote, any claim
about her record that isn't sourced.

---

## 4. Proof, so the contrast lands as credible (not just rhetoric)

Voters discount challengers who only assert difference. Back each pillar with a *visible behavior*
the incumbent's posture can't easily match:

- **Accessibility:** publish a standing town-hall calendar across every county in the current map
  (verify which map is in legal effect — see `candidate/data-and-map-plan.md`). Showing up *is* the contrast.
- **Term limits:** sign a public self-imposed term-limit pledge. The act, not the ad, is the message.
- **Children first:** the CHILD Protection Act one-pager — a concrete bill no one else is carrying.
- **Results:** a short record of coalition wins from his legal career (sourced, candidate-approved).

---

## 5. Audience routing (where the unnamed contrast does the most work)

- **New-map rural counties** (Jefferson/Washington/Crawford/Gasconade): lead with
  "shows up everywhere, not just the wealthy suburbs" — accessibility + district-first, no name.
- **Suburban St. Louis County Republicans:** lead with results + affordability discipline.
- **First-time primary voters:** lead with fresh energy + term limits.
- **Parents / family-court-touched voters:** lead with the CHILD Protection Act (his alone).

---

## 6. Operating rules for staff

1. The opponent is never the subject of a sentence. Ever.
2. Category words ("career politicians," "more than a decade") are allowed, factual, and rationed.
3. Every record-based claim carries a `source_url` or it doesn't ship (config §5/§7).
4. Lead with the promise; let the gap speak.
5. When in doubt, swap an attack for a higher standard Matt is willing to be held to.
